01 The problem

Ranking candidates isn't the hard part anymore

Drug repurposing means finding a new use for a medicine that's already known to be safe in humans — which skips years of safety testing that a brand-new molecule would need. Models that suggest "this drug might hit this protein" are common. The bottleneck is what happens after the list comes out.

What a typical predictor gives you
  • A single confidence number, e.g. 0.73
  • No signal for whether that number is trustworthy on this particular protein
  • You check the literature yourself — and people naturally search for evidence that confirms what they already suspect
  • The statistically weakest predictions get challenged the least, in practice

What TrustDTI gives you
  • A prediction plus a statistically calibrated trust category — not just a raw number
  • A curated (never predicted) check on whether the target protein is genuinely disease-linked
  • An agent that deliberately flips its search strategy: hunts for support when confident, hunts for contradictions when unsure
  • A traceable report — every claim points back to a real citation
02 What's actually new here

Said plainly, before anyone else says it

Being upfront about what's not invented here is what makes the one real claim credible. Tap each row.

A very active, crowded research area. Predicting whether a compound binds a protein from its structure/sequence is done by hundreds of published models. TrustDTI's fusion head only needs to be good enough and rigorously validated — it deliberately does not chase state-of-the-art benchmark performance, because the contribution sits downstream of it.
Wrapping a predictor in split conformal prediction to get a statistically guaranteed confidence set is already published elsewhere for this exact task. TrustDTI uses the technique, it doesn't invent it.
An established subfield of retrieval-augmented generation — systems that search harder or differently when they're unsure. The pattern exists; it's usually applied to a system reasoning about its own confidence in an answer it's about to give.
Close prior work already exists on AI agents that go gather literature evidence to support a repurposing hypothesis.
the one actual claim

Nobody has directly tested using an externally calibrated statistical signal from one system (a molecular prediction model) to control the search behaviour of a completely different system (a literature-retrieval agent) — as opposed to a system reasoning about its own self-confidence. That narrow, specific cross-paradigm bridge is what this project tests. A literature re-audit against the newest work happens in Week 9, before this claim is finalised in writing.

04 Two networks — don't mix them up

One is predicted. One is looked up. Never store them the same way.

TrustDTI builds two related but structurally different objects. The briefing calls confusing them "the trap to avoid" — it would break the entire disease-anchor design at its root. Tap a card to flip it.

Network 1

Compound ↔ Protein

Nodes: compounds and proteins only. One edge type: a measured or predicted interaction, carrying a probability and a conformal prediction set. Built in Weeks 1–6; trains the fusion head.

tap to flip →

This is the network the AI model actually learns. Every edge here is something a neural network produced — which is exactly why it needs a calibrated trust score attached, and why it must never be confused with a fact looked up from a database.

Network 2

Compound ↔ Protein ↔ Disease

Adds disease nodes and a second, structurally different edge type: a curated protein–disease association pulled from Open Targets / DISGENET. Built in Week 8, on top of the finished Network 1.

tap to flip →

This edge is never predicted by any model in this project — it's a fact looked up from a human-curated biology database, with its own evidence score. Keeping it separate keeps the "is this protein disease-relevant" question auditable and independent of anything the AI produced.

the trap, specifically

If predicted compound–protein edges and curated protein–disease edges ever land in one undifferentiated table, the system loses the distinction its whole design depends on. Rule: store them as two separate tables, or two edge types with an explicit provenance column ("predicted" vs "curated") — from the very first script that touches them.

07 Evidence categories & the trickiest rule in the project

Four kinds of evidence, three directions, one dangerous shortcut

Every category mirrors a step a pharmacologist actually reasons through:

Mechanistic

Does the drug actually affect this protein? (binding, inhibition)

Disease context

Does this protein matter for this disease? (pathway, genetics)

Counter

What would make this prediction wrong? (failed assays, contradictions)

Safety

Even if it works — reason not to pursue it? (toxicity, terminated trials)

Every retrieved passage becomes a record with a direction: SUPPORT, CONTRADICT, or UNKNOWN. Try the scenario below — it's the single easiest mistake to make in this whole system.

The agent searches thoroughly for literature on Drug X and Protein Y. It finds nothing relevant at all — no supporting studies, no contradicting studies, nothing. What direction should this record get?
08 Two data rules that protect everything downstream

Get these wrong and every later result is quietly poisoned

These apply the moment raw bioactivity data is touched — long before any model is trained.

ACTIVE

A measurement exists and shows meaningful activity.

INACTIVE

A measurement exists and shows no meaningful activity under the tested conditions.

UNTESTED

No measurement exists. Never silently turned into INACTIVE — most possible pairs were simply never tried.

Why this matters: in a sparse network like this, almost every possible drug–protein pair is untested. Treat "never measured" as "measured and negative" and you teach the model that almost nothing binds — destroying precision on exactly the novel candidates the project exists to surface.

Rule 2 — censored ≠ exact

A real row from the actual data

activity_id 884645 · imatinib vs. FLT3 · standard_type IC50 · standard_relation > · standard_value 30,000 nM

Read literally, that > means: "the test was stopped at 30 micromolar without finding where the drug stops working" — a lower bound, not a precise measurement of exactly 30,000 nM. Treating it as an exact number would be a real error, not a nitpick.

Here's what happens to imatinib's own activity records if you take the tempting shortcut of keeping only rows that have a clean potency value:

all rows: 4,878
kept by the shortcut: 833 (17%)

The shortcut throws away all 1,335 censored rows — which are exactly the strongest "this doesn't work" evidence available.

09 The two things that must never quietly change

Everything else in this project is allowed to evolve. These aren't.

◈ The research question

"Does conditioning literature-evidence retrieval on calibrated prediction uncertainty (plus a disease-relevance signal) improve evidence precision and counter-evidence recall, compared with a fixed retrieval policy applied to the same candidates?"

Written so it can fail. If uncertainty-adaptive retrieval doesn't beat the boring fixed policy, that's the finding — reported honestly, never adjusted until it looks better.

🔒 The blinding protocol

Six retrospective test cases get frozen into a sealed file in Week 8 and must not be read, referenced, or allowed to influence any decision until a specific unsealing point in Week 9.

This is a blinding protocol. Breaking it silently — even peeking — would invalidate the project's central experiment, the same way peeking at exam answers invalidates the exam.
